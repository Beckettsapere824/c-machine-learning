# M3.2 Hard-Margin Dual Derivation

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M3.1 Hard-Margin Primal](M3.1.md), [M0.11 Lagrange Multipliers](M0.11.md)

> **Interactive demo**: [Hard-margin SVM geometry](https://beckettsapere824.github.io/ml-demos/svm-margin.html) — the yellow-ring points are exactly those with $\alpha_i>0$.

---

## Content / 内容

We start from the primal
$$
\min_{\mathbf{w},b}\;\tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)\ge1\quad\forall i.
$$
Introduce a non-negative multiplier $\alpha_i$ for each inequality and form the Lagrangian
$$
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w},b,\boldsymbol{\alpha})
= \tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 + \sum_i\alpha_i\bigl(1-y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)\bigr).
$$

### Stationarity (the only algebra you need)

Differentiate with respect to the primal variables and set the derivatives to zero (node [M0.11](M0.11.md)):
$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{w}-\sum_i\alpha_i y_i\mathbf{x}_i=0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mathbf{w}=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i\mathbf{x}_i, \tag{*}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial b}=-\sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0. \tag{**}
$$
Equation (*) already tells us the most important geometric fact: **the optimal weight is a linear combination of the training points**, and only those points whose $\alpha_i$ is positive (the support vectors) receive a non-zero coefficient.

### Obtaining the dual objective

Substitute (*) back into $\mathcal{L}$ and use (**) to eliminate the terms that contain $b$. After a short rearrangement one obtains the dual function
$$
g(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\sum_i\alpha_i - \tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j\langle\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_j\rangle.
$$
The dual problem is therefore the concave quadratic programme
$$
\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\;g(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\alpha_i\ge0,\quad\sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0.
$$

### Recovering $b$

Any support vector that lies exactly on the margin ($0<\alpha_i$) satisfies $y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)=1$, which can be solved for $b$. In practice one averages over all such free support vectors for numerical stability.

对偶推导的完整路径：写拉格朗日 → 对 $\mathbf{w},b$ 求驻点 → 得到 $\mathbf{w}=\sum\alpha_i y_i\mathbf{x}_i$ → 代回得到只含内积的对偶目标。支持向量 = $\alpha_i>0$ 的样本。

## Why the dual is useful

1. The dual depends on the data only through inner products → the door to the kernel trick (M3.4).
2. The solution is sparse: most $\alpha_i$ are zero, so prediction only needs the support vectors.
3. The box constraints that appear in the soft-margin dual ($0\le\alpha_i\le C$) are easy to handle with coordinate descent.

## Tiny Python sketch (after the dual QP is solved)
```python
w = (alpha * y) @ X          # X has shape (n, d)
sv = alpha > 1e-5
b = np.mean(y[sv] - X[sv] @ w)
```

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

- Forgetting the constraint $\sum\alpha_i y_i=0$ (it comes from $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial b=0$).
- Thinking every training point is a support vector; in a typical problem only a small fraction have $\alpha_i>0$.
- Mixing up the sign in the Lagrangian (the constraint is written as $1-y_i(\dots)\le0$, so the multiplier term carries a plus).

## Practice / 练习

Why does the dual constraint $\sum\alpha_i y_i=0$ appear? What happens if it is omitted?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

It is exactly the stationarity condition $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial b=0$. Omitting it leaves the Lagrangian unbounded below when $b$ is sent to $\pm\infty$.

</details>
