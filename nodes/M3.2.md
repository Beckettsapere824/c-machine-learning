# M3.2 Hard-Margin Dual Derivation

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M3.1 Hard-Margin Primal](M3.1.md), [M0.11 Lagrange Multipliers](M0.11.md)

---

## Content / 内容

Introduce a multiplier $\alpha_i\ge0$ for each inequality constraint and form the Lagrangian
$$
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w},b,\boldsymbol{\alpha})
= \tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 + \sum_i\alpha_i\bigl(1-y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)\bigr).
$$
Stationarity with respect to the primal variables yields
$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{w}-\sum_i\alpha_i y_i\mathbf{x}_i=0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mathbf{w}=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i\mathbf{x}_i,
$$
$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial b}=-\sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0.
$$
Substitute these relations back into $\mathcal{L}$ to obtain the dual objective
$$
g(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\sum_i\alpha_i - \tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j\langle\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_j\rangle,
$$
which must be maximised subject to $\alpha_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0$.  
Only those training points with $\alpha_i>0$ (the **support vectors**) appear in the expansion of $\mathbf{w}$. The bias $b$ is recovered from any support vector by the KKT complementarity condition $y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)=1$.

对偶推导：写拉格朗日 → 对 $\mathbf{w},b$ 求驻点 → 代回得到只含内积的二次型。支持向量对应 $\alpha_i>0$ 的样本。

**Link to M0.11**  
Every algebraic step above is an instance of the general Lagrange-multiplier recipe already practised in the pure-math node.

## Tiny Python sketch
```python
# after solving the dual QP for alpha
w = (alpha * y) @ X          # X rows are training points
# b from any support vector
sv = alpha > 1e-5
b = np.mean(y[sv] - X[sv]@w)
```

## Practice / 练习

Why does the dual constraint $\sum\alpha_i y_i=0$ appear? What happens if it is omitted?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

It is exactly the stationarity condition $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial b=0$. Omitting it would leave the Lagrangian unbounded below in the $b$ direction.

</details>
