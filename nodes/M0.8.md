## Content / 内容

Matrix calculus is just ordinary multivariable calculus written in compact vector/matrix notation. Once you memorise three identities, almost every gradient that appears in linear models, SVMs and neural nets becomes a one-line application.

矩阵微积分只是把普通多元微积分写成向量/矩阵的紧凑形式。记住下面三条恒等式后，线性模型、SVM、神经网络里出现的绝大多数梯度都能一行写出来。

### The three workhorse identities

Let $\mathbf{a}$ be a constant vector, $A$ a constant matrix, $\mathbf{x}$ the variable.

$$
\begin{align*}
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{a}^\top\mathbf{x}) &= \mathbf{a}, \tag{1} \\
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x}^\top A\mathbf{x}) &= (A+A^\top)\mathbf{x}
\quad\text{(if }A\text{ is symmetric: }2A\mathbf{x}), \tag{2} \\
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\|A\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}\|_2^2 &= 2A^\top(A\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}). \tag{3}
\end{align*}
$$

### Where do they come from? (short derivations)

**Identity (1)**  
$\mathbf{a}^\top\mathbf{x}=\sum_i a_i x_i$. The partial derivative w.r.t. $x_k$ is simply $a_k$. Stacking them gives $\mathbf{a}$.

**Identity (2)**  
Expand the quadratic form:
$$
\mathbf{x}^\top A\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i,j}x_i A_{ij}x_j.
$$
Differentiate w.r.t. $x_k$:
$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_k}=\sum_j A_{kj}x_j + \sum_i x_i A_{ik}=(A\mathbf{x})_k+(A^\top\mathbf{x})_k.
$$
Hence the gradient is $(A+A^\top)\mathbf{x}$. When $A$ is symmetric this collapses to $2A\mathbf{x}$.

**Identity (3)**  
Let $\mathbf{r}=A\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}$. Then $\|\mathbf{r}\|_2^2=\mathbf{r}^\top\mathbf{r}$.  
By the chain rule and (1)–(2):
$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{r}^\top\mathbf{r})=2\,(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{r})^\top\mathbf{r}=2A^\top\mathbf{r}=2A^\top(A\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}).
$$

### Chain rule in vector form

If $g:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^m$ and $f:\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}$, then
$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f = (Dg)^\top\nabla_g f,
$$
where $Dg$ is the $m\times d$ Jacobian of $g$. This is exactly the local rule used by back-propagation (node M8.3).

## Concrete walk-through / 具体演算

**Least-squares gradient (the one you will write a hundred times)**

$$
J(\mathbf{w})=\frac12\|X\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}\|_2^2.
$$
Apply identity (3) with $A=X$, $\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{y}$, and drop the constant factor 2 because of the $\frac12$:
$$
\nabla J = X^\top(X\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}).
$$
Set it to zero → normal equations $X^\top X\mathbf{w}=X^\top\mathbf{y}$ (node M2.2).

**Logistic-regression gradient (preview of M2.7)**

The negative log-likelihood involves $\sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i)$. After the scalar chain rule the residual $(\sigma_i-y_i)$ appears, and the vector chain rule then multiplies by $\mathbf{x}_i$. Summing over $i$ again yields the matrix form $X^\top(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathbf{y})$.

## Tiny Python verification
```python
import numpy as np
X = np.random.randn(5, 3)
y = np.random.randn(5)
w = np.random.randn(3)

def J(w):
    return 0.5 * np.sum((X @ w - y) ** 2)

eps = 1e-6
grad_num = np.array([(J(w + eps * e) - J(w)) / eps for e in np.eye(3)])
grad_ana = X.T @ (X @ w - y)
print(np.allclose(grad_num, grad_ana))   # True
```

## Common pitfalls / 易错点

- Forgetting the factor 2 when $A$ is not symmetric, or when the $\frac12$ is missing from the loss.
- Mixing up left/right multiplication: $\nabla(\mathbf{x}^\top A)=A^\top$ (not $A$) if the result is regarded as a column gradient.
- Applying identity (3) to $\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$ without noticing that $A=I$, $\mathbf{b}=0$.

## Practice / 练习

1. Derive $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\frac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$ from identity (2).  
2. Derive the gradient of the ridge objective $\frac12\|X\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}\|_2^2+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

1. $\frac12\mathbf{w}^\top I\mathbf{w}$ → identity (2) with $A=I$ → $\nabla=\mathbf{w}$.  
2. $\nabla = X^\top(X\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}) + \lambda\mathbf{w}$.

</details>
