# M4.1 Covariance Matrix & Empirical Second-Moment

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M0.3 Matrices](M0.3.md), [M0.6 Positive-Definite Matrices](M0.6.md)

---

## Content / 内容

Given a centred data matrix $\tilde{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ (each column has mean zero) the **empirical covariance matrix** is
$$
\Sigma = \frac1n\tilde{X}^\top\tilde{X}
\quad\text{(or }\tfrac1{n-1}\text{ for the unbiased version)}.
$$
$\Sigma$ is always symmetric and positive semi-definite. Its $(j,k)$-entry is the sample covariance between features $j$ and $k$. The quadratic form
$$
\mathbf{u}^\top\Sigma\mathbf{u} = \frac1n\|\tilde{X}\mathbf{u}\|_2^2
$$
is exactly the empirical variance of the scalar projections of the data onto the direction $\mathbf{u}$.

中心化数据矩阵的协方差 $\Sigma=\frac1n\tilde{X}^\top\tilde{X}$ 半正定。$\mathbf{u}^\top\Sigma\mathbf{u}$ 就是数据在方向 $\mathbf{u}$ 上的样本方差——PCA 的目标函数正是它。

## Tiny Python sketch
```python
X_c = X - X.mean(axis=0)
Sigma = (X_c.T @ X_c) / X.shape[0]
# or simply np.cov(X, rowvar=False)
```

## Practice / 练习

Prove that $\Sigma\succeq0$ without invoking the spectral theorem.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

$\mathbf{u}^\top\Sigma\mathbf{u}=\frac1n\|\tilde{X}\mathbf{u}\|_2^2\ge0$ for every $\mathbf{u}$.

</details>
