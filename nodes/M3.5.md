# M3.5 Representer Theorem intuition & Kernel Decision Function

**Prerequisites / 前置**: [M3.4 Kernel Trick](M3.4.md)

## Content / 内容

Even though the feature map $\phi$ may land in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, the optimal weight vector of a regularised risk functional always lies in the finite-dimensional span of the training mapped points:
$$
\mathbf{w}=\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i\phi(\mathbf{x}_i)
$$
(the **representer theorem**). Consequently the decision function can be written solely in terms of kernel evaluations:
$$
f(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i y_i k(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x})+b.
$$
Only the support vectors (those with $\alpha_i\ne0$) contribute to the sum, giving the sparse expansion that makes SVM prediction efficient.

表示定理保证最优解总在训练样本映射后的张成空间内，因此决策函数只需核函数求值，且只依赖支持向量。

## Practical consequence / 实际后果

Prediction cost is $O(\#\text{support vectors}\times\text{cost of }k)$. For the RBF kernel this is still linear in the number of support vectors; for very large data sets one therefore resorts to budgeted or Nyström approximations.

## Practice / 练习

Why does the representer theorem fail if the regulariser is not a strictly increasing function of the Hilbert-space norm?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

The proof relies on decomposing any candidate $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}_\parallel+\mathbf{w}_\perp$ where $\mathbf{w}_\parallel$ lies in the span of the $\phi(\mathbf{x}_i)$. The regulariser penalises $\|\mathbf{w}_\perp\|$ while the data term is unchanged by $\mathbf{w}_\perp$; hence the optimum must have $\mathbf{w}_\perp=0$. Without a norm-based regulariser this argument disappears.

</details>
