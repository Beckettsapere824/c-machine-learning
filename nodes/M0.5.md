## Content / 内容

A non-zero vector $\mathbf{v}$ is an **eigenvector** of the square matrix $A$ with **eigenvalue** $\lambda$ when
$$
A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}.
$$
The eigenvalues are the roots of the characteristic polynomial $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$.  
For a real **symmetric** matrix the eigenvalues are always real and there exists an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors:
$$
A=Q\Lambda Q^\top,
$$
where the columns of $Q$ are the eigenvectors and $\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_d)$. This is the **spectral theorem**.

The **Rayleigh quotient**
$$
R(\mathbf{u})=\frac{\mathbf{u}^\top A\mathbf{u}}{\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{u}}
$$
has maximum $\lambda_{\max}$ and minimum $\lambda_{\min}$ attained at the corresponding eigenvectors.

对称矩阵一定可正交对角化。Rayleigh 商的最大/最小值就是最大/最小特征值——PCA 的目标函数正是这个商。

**Why this matters later**  
PCA is literally the spectral decomposition of the covariance matrix. LDA solves a generalised eigenproblem $S_B\mathbf{u}=\lambda S_W\mathbf{u}$. Understanding the spectral theorem removes all mystery from those algorithms.

## Example / 例子

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}
$$
has eigenvalues $3$ and $1$ with orthonormal eigenvectors $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1)^\top$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,-1)^\top$.

## Tiny Python sketch
```python
A = np.array([[2.,1.],[1.,2.]])
lam, Q = np.linalg.eigh(A)   # for symmetric matrices prefer eigh
print(lam)                   # [1. 3.]
print(Q)
```

## Practice / 练习

Show that if $A$ is symmetric and $\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}$ are eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues, then $\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle=0$.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

$\lambda\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle=\langle A\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{u},A\mathbf{v}\rangle=\mu\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle$. Since $\lambda\ne\mu$ the inner product must vanish.

</details>
