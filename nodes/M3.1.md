## Content / 内容

A hyperplane $\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}+b=0$ partitions $\mathbb{R}^d$ into two half-spaces. The (geometric) margin of a labelled point $(\mathbf{x}_i,y_i)$ is the signed distance
$$
\gamma_i = y_i\frac{\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b}{\|\mathbf{w}\|_2}.
$$
The margin of the whole data set is $\gamma=\min_i\gamma_i$. The **hard-margin SVM** seeks the hyperplane that maximises $\gamma$, which is equivalent to the convex quadratic programme
$$
\begin{align*}
\min_{\mathbf{w},b}\quad & \tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 \\
\text{s.t.}\quad & y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)\ge1,\quad i=1,\dots,n.
\end{align*}
$$
(The factor $\tfrac12$ and the normalisation “≥1” are chosen so that the optimal margin equals $1/\|\mathbf{w}\|_2$.)

硬间隔 SVM 最大化几何间隔，等价于在所有点都被正确分类且函数间隔至少为 1 的约束下最小化 $\frac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$。

**Why the quadratic objective?**  
Maximising $1/\|\mathbf{w}\|_2$ is the same as minimising $\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$; the square keeps the problem smooth and strongly convex.

## Geometric picture / 几何直观

The two parallel supporting hyperplanes $\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}+b=\pm1$ touch the convex hulls of the two classes. The slab between them is the margin; its width is $2/\|\mathbf{w}\|_2$. Support vectors are the points that lie on the boundary of the slab.

## Practice / 练习

Show that if $(\mathbf{w},b)$ is feasible then so is $(\lambda\mathbf{w},\lambda b)$ for any $\lambda>0$, yet the objective changes. Why does the constraint “≥1” remove this scaling freedom?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

Scaling multiplies all functional margins by $\lambda$. Forcing the smallest functional margin to be exactly 1 fixes the scale and makes the geometric margin equal to $1/\|\mathbf{w}\|_2$.

</details>
