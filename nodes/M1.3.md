## Content / 内容

In high dimension the volume of a ball concentrates near its surface and pairwise distances become almost equal. Formally, for i.i.d. points drawn from a distribution with bounded support in $\mathbb{R}^d$,
$$
\frac{\max_i d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_i)-\min_i d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_i)}{\min_i d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_i)}\to 0
\quad\text{as }d\to\infty.
$$
Consequently the notion of “nearest” neighbour loses discriminative power—the **curse of dimensionality**.  
Practical consequences for $k$-NN:
- exponential growth of the number of samples needed to keep the same density;
- distance concentration makes both Euclidean and Manhattan metrics less useful;
- raw pixel vectors of $100\times100$ face images live in $10^4$-dimensional space and are essentially unusable by plain $k$-NN.

A typical classical face-recognition pipeline therefore inserts a dimensionality-reduction stage (PCA or LDA, later nodes) before the nearest-neighbour classifier:
$$
\text{detect}\to\text{align}\to\text{vectorise}\to\text{PCA/LDA}\to k\text{-NN}.
$$

高维中距离趋于相等，“最近”失去意义。人脸图像的原始像素维度太高，必须先降维再送给 $k$-NN。这正是后续 PCA/LDA 节点存在的工程动机。

## Concrete numbers / 具体数字

A $100\times100$ grey-scale face is a vector in $\mathbb{R}^{10000}$.  
Even 10 000 training faces give on average only one point per unit hyper-cube of side length 1—hopelessly sparse. After projecting onto the first 100 principal components the same data become dense enough for $k$-NN to work.

## Tiny Python illustration of distance concentration
```python
def dist_ratio(d, n=1000):
    X = np.random.randn(n, d)
    q  = np.random.randn(d)
    dists = np.linalg.norm(X-q, axis=1)
    return (dists.max()-dists.min())/dists.min()

print([dist_ratio(d) for d in [2,10,100,1000]])
# typical output: [~2.5, ~0.8, ~0.2, ~0.05]  → concentration
```
