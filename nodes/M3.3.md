## Content / 内容

When the data are not linearly separable we allow functional margins smaller than 1 by introducing non-negative slack variables $\xi_i$:
$$
\begin{align*}
\min_{\mathbf{w},b,\boldsymbol{\xi}}\quad
& \tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 + C\sum_i\xi_i \\
\text{s.t.}\quad
& y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)\ge1-\xi_i,\quad\xi_i\ge0.
\end{align*}
$$
The hyper-parameter $C>0$ trades off margin size against total slack.  
The dual becomes the same quadratic programme as the hard-margin case, except that the multipliers are now boxed:
$$
0\le\alpha_i\le C.
$$
KKT complementary slackness implies:
- $\alpha_i=0$ → point is outside the margin and correctly classified;
- $0<\alpha_i<C$ → point lies exactly on the margin (support vector);
- $\alpha_i=C$ → point is inside the margin or misclassified (also a support vector).

软间隔引入松弛变量 $\xi_i$，目标多一项 $C\sum\xi_i$。对偶中 $\alpha_i$ 被限制在 $[0,C]$。$C$ 控制“间隔”与“容忍错分”的权衡。

**Hinge-loss view**  
Eliminating the slacks shows that the primal is equivalent to minimising
$$
\tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 + C\sum_i\max\bigl(0,1-y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}_i+b)\bigr),
$$
the classic regularised hinge loss.

## Tiny Python sketch
```python
from sklearn.svm import SVC
clf = SVC(kernel='linear', C=1.0)
clf.fit(X, y)
print(clf.support_vectors_)
```

## Practice / 练习

What happens to the soft-margin solution as $C\to\infty$? As $C\to0$?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

$C\to\infty$ recovers the hard-margin SVM (if the data are separable).  
$C\to0$ forces $\mathbf{w}\to0$; the decision function becomes constant and the margin constraint is ignored.

</details>
