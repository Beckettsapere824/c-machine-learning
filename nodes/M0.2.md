## Content / 内容

A **norm** $\|\cdot\|$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ satisfies positivity, homogeneity and the triangle inequality. The three most common norms are
$$
\|\mathbf{x}\|_1 = \sum_i |x_i|,\qquad
\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_i x_i^2},\qquad
\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty = \max_i |x_i|.
$$
They induce distances $d_p(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_p$.  
In finite dimension all norms are equivalent (they give the same topology), but the **geometry of unit balls** is radically different: the $\ell_2$ ball is a sphere, the $\ell_1$ ball is a diamond, the $\ell_\infty$ ball is a cube. Consequently the set of nearest neighbours of a query point can change when the norm is changed.

范数满足正定、齐次、三角不等式。三种常用范数对应不同的单位球形状，因此会改变最近邻的判定结果。有限维中所有范数拓扑等价，但几何形状差异巨大。

**Why this matters later**  
$k$-NN with Euclidean distance versus Manhattan distance can produce different decision boundaries. Regularisation terms $\|\mathbf{w}\|_1$ (Lasso) and $\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$ (Ridge) also come from these norms.

## Example / 例子

Points $\mathbf{a}=(0,0)$, $\mathbf{b}=(1,1)$, $\mathbf{c}=(2,0)$.  
- $\ell_2$: $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\sqrt{2}\approx1.41$, $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})=2$ → $\mathbf{b}$ closer.  
- $\ell_1$: $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=2$, $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})=2$ → equidistant.  
- $\ell_\infty$: $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=1$, $d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})=2$ → $\mathbf{b}$ closer.

## Tiny Python sketch
```python
from numpy.linalg import norm
a, b, c = np.array([0.,0.]), np.array([1.,1.]), np.array([2.,0.])
print(norm(a-b,1), norm(a-c,1))   # 2.0 2.0
print(norm(a-b,2), norm(a-c,2))   # 1.41 2.0
print(norm(a-b,np.inf), norm(a-c,np.inf))  # 1.0 2.0
```

## Practice / 练习

For the three points above, which point is the nearest neighbour of $(1,0)$ under each of the three norms?

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

Under $\ell_2$ and $\ell_\infty$ the nearest is $(0,0)$ or $(2,0)$ (both distance 1); under $\ell_1$ all three points are at distance 1 from $(1,0)$.

</details>
