# M0.1 Vectors, Inner Product, Euclidean Geometry

**Prerequisites / 前置**: none

---

## Content / 内容

A vector in $\mathbb{R}^d$ is written as a column
$$
\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_d \end{pmatrix}.
$$
Addition and scalar multiplication are component-wise. The **Euclidean inner product** is
$$
\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle = \mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{y} = \sum_{i=1}^d x_i y_i.
$$
It induces the Euclidean length (norm)
$$
\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = \sqrt{\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x} \rangle}.
$$
The angle $\theta$ between two non-zero vectors satisfies
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 \|\mathbf{y}\|_2}.
$$
Two vectors are orthogonal when $\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle = 0$. The orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{y}$ onto the line spanned by $\mathbf{x}$ is
$$
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{y} = \frac{\langle \mathbf{y}, \mathbf{x} \rangle}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_2^2}\mathbf{x}.
$$

向量是列向量。内积 $\langle \mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle=\mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{y}$ 诱导欧氏长度 $\|\mathbf{x}\|_2$。夹角余弦公式与正交投影公式是后续所有“距离”和“投影”计算的起点。

**Why this matters later**  
- Every distance used by $k$-NN is built from a norm.  
- The margin of an SVM is $2/\|\mathbf{w}\|_2$.  
- PCA finds directions of maximum variance measured by $\mathbf{u}^\top\Sigma\mathbf{u}$.  
If the geometry of the inner product is unclear, all later formulas become pure algebra without meaning.

## Example / 例子

Let $\mathbf{x}=(3,4)^\top$, $\mathbf{y}=(1,0)^\top$.  
$$\|\mathbf{x}\|_2=5,\quad \langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle=3,\quad \cos\theta=3/5.$$
Projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto $\mathbf{y}$ is $(3,0)^\top$.

## Tiny Python sketch
```python
import numpy as np
x = np.array([3., 4.])
y = np.array([1., 0.])
print(np.dot(x, y))          # 3.0
print(np.linalg.norm(x))     # 5.0
print(np.dot(x, y) / (np.linalg.norm(x)*np.linalg.norm(y)))  # cosθ
```

## Practice / 练习

Compute the orthogonal projection of $(2,3,1)^\top$ onto $(1,1,0)^\top$.

<details>
<summary>Solution sketch</summary>

$\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle=5$, $\|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2=2$, so projection $=\frac{5}{2}(1,1,0)^\top=(2.5,2.5,0)^\top$.

</details>
